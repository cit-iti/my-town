Flint, Michigan

- The largest city and county seat of Genesee County, along the Flint River,
  about 66 miles northwest of Detroit. Population was 81,252 at the 2020 census,
  the 12th-largest city in Michigan.
- Founded as a village by fur trader Jacob Smith in 1819 and incorporated as a
  city in 1855.
- Nicknamed "Vehicle City": it was a major carriage manufacturer in the 1800s
  and became an automobile powerhouse after General Motors was founded there
  by William Durant in 1908.
- The 1936-37 sit-down strike at GM plants in Flint was a turning point in the
  U.S. labor movement and led to recognition of the United Auto Workers union.
- Population peaked at nearly 197,000 in 1960, then declined sharply as GM
  reduced operations from the late 1960s onward.
